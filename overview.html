Overview.

The framework behind our work: three nested scales of the built environment, four i's of inquiry, and a lifecycle from planning to regeneration.

01 / Built Environment

From a single building to an entire city.

City
Campus
Building

Three nested scales of research.

The built environment isn't a single artifact — it's a set of nested systems. Our research moves fluidly between them, because decisions at each scale shape the others.

BE · 01

Building

Housing and individual structures — performance of the envelope, and the quality of the spaces within.

BE · 02

Campus

Universities and institutional precincts — coordinated portfolios of buildings and open space.

BE · 03

City

Urban systems, infrastructure networks, and the built environment at metropolitan scale.

02 / The Four i's

Four pillars of inquiry, tied by a shared purpose.

i — 01

Informatics

Structured knowledge of the built environment — data models, spatial reasoning, and the representations that let machines understand architecture.

BIM GIS Knowledge Graphs
i — 02

Intelligence

Machine learning, reasoning, and decision support — helping designers, operators, and cities make better choices under real-world uncertainty.

Machine Learning Decision Support AI
i — 03

Industrialization

Turning ideas into matter — robotics, modular construction, and the industrialization of how buildings get made at scale and with precision.

Robotics Modular Construction
i — 04

Integration

Systems thinking across people, processes, and platforms — collaboration, participation, and the dynamics that hold the other three i's together.

Collaboration Participation System Dynamics

04 / The Research Matrix

A simple formula for complex work.

BE × i × LM
Three scales · Four methods · Three lifecycle phases · 36 research positions

Every project in the lab sits somewhere on this grid — a specific combination of method, scale, and lifecycle phase. It's a compass for new research, a way to explain what we do to partners, and an honest map of where knowledge is still thin.
